Story 1 · Clinical development

From Phase II evidence to a probability of success

Servier France
Data Scientist (Biostatistics) intern
Onco Late Phase statistics team
May to September 2026 · Saclay

ContextQuestionObstacleChoicesWorkChecksOutcomeLimits
01Context

Moving an oncology drug from Phase II to Phase III is a costly decision in clinical development. At that point, the evidence is usually thin: a few small Phase II studies, sometimes with a single treatment arm, plus results from other trials run in different settings.

A probability of success (PoS) summarises what this evidence says about the chance that a future Phase III will meet its statistical success criterion. It is a decision aid for the statistics team, not a prediction of approval or clinical benefit.

02Question

How can the evidence available at the end of Phase II give an honest probability of success for a future Phase III, when that evidence is small, single-arm, heterogeneous and not directly comparable?

03Obstacle

Three sources of uncertainty stack up, and ignoring any one of them makes the PoS look more certain than it is.

04Choices

No meeting notes are published here. The points below are the methodological questions the framework had to answer, except the last one, which is documented.

How much should external studies count?methodological question

Tension
Borrowing more information narrows uncertainty, but non-comparable studies can bias the result.
Choice
Meta-Analytic-Predictive (MAP) priors in a hierarchical model, so the amount borrowed depends on how much the studies disagree.

What do we do with single-arm Phase II studies?methodological question

Tension
Without a concurrent control arm, the treatment effect depends on an external comparison.
Choice
Treat that comparison as uncertain evidence inside the model, rather than as a fixed reference value.

How do we move from Phase II to Phase III?methodological question

Tension
Population, design or endpoint can change between phases.
Choice
Compute the PoS from the predictive distribution of the future trial, not from the posterior of the Phase II effect.

Does the answer survive other assumptions?methodological question

Tension
A PoS is sensitive to priors and design hypotheses.
Choice
Sensitivity analyses, and an app in which those assumptions can be changed and their effect on the PoS seen directly.

Final presentation to the Onco Late Phase teamdocumented

Outcome
The work was presented to the team, with questions from the statisticians. The supervisor's recommendation describes the presentation as clear and structured.
05Work
  1. Framed the problem across continuous, binary and time-to-event endpoints, the three endpoint families a PoS has to handle.
  2. Built hierarchical Bayesian models with MAP priors to combine the target study with external evidence.
  3. Implemented the inference in Stan (MCMC with Hamiltonian Monte Carlo and NUTS), because the models have no closed-form posterior.
  4. Propagated the uncertainty from the posterior to the predictive distribution of a future Phase III, then to the PoS.
  5. Delivered an R Shiny application with a layered architecture and unit tests, to explore evidence and design hypotheses and their effect on the PoS.
  6. Presented the framework to the Onco Late Phase team.
06Checks
  • ConvergenceMCMC diagnostics, so that the estimated PoS reflects the model and not a sampler that has not converged. Sampling well does not make a model right, which is why the next check exists.
  • SensitivitySensitivity analyses on the assumptions that drive borrowing and extrapolation.
  • SoftwareUnit tests and a layered architecture for the application.
  • ReviewPresentation and questions from the Onco Late Phase statisticians.
07Outcome

Delivered: a Bayesian PoS framework that accounts for external-data comparability, between-study heterogeneity and extrapolation to a future trial, and a deployed, tested R Shiny application to explore it.

What this is not: an approval forecast, a clinical-benefit claim, or evidence that a specific development decision was taken because of it.

08Limits

What remains open

  • A PoS is only as credible as the comparability assumptions behind the external data.
  • Study data and project results are confidential, so they are not shown here.

What I took from it

  • Separate uncertainty about a parameter from uncertainty about a future trial.
  • Build tools that make assumptions visible, so non-specialists can question them.

Story 2 · Public health

Did community health workers lighten the load on health centres?

Centre MURAZ (INSP Burkina Faso)
Data Science intern · REACT2 project
January to April 2024 · Bobo-Dioulasso
Study data: June 2021 to Dec. 2023

ContextQuestionObstacleChoicesWorkChecksOutcomeLimits
01Context

Malaria is the first reason for medical consultations in Burkina Faso. In the Diébougou health district, in the south-west, many villages are far from a health centre. The REACT2 project trained community health workers (CHWs), supervised by nurses, to test, treat and raise awareness directly in 18 villages.

02Question

Once a village had community health workers, did fewer malaria cases reach its health centre?

03Obstacle

A simple before/after comparison would mix the programme with the seasons: malaria rises with the rains whatever happens. The study design helps. Villages entered the programme in three randomised waves, so at any date some villages are covered and others are not yet.

04Choices

What do we do with a village whose records are incomplete?documented

Tension
Keeping all 18 villages, or keeping series that can be trusted.
Choice
The gaps found during cleaning were reported to the supervisors. The village was finally excluded: 17 villages and 2,295 village-weeks were analysed.

Is a Poisson model enough for these counts?methodological question

Tension
Poisson assumes the variance equals the mean. Here the dispersion ratio was 1.53 (p < 0.001).
Choice
Negative-binomial models, compared by AICc: type II fitted best (figure below).

Which weather variables should enter the model?methodological question

Tension
Eleven satellite weather variables, strongly correlated with each other.
Choice
A PCA kept the best-represented ones: temperature, specific humidity and wind speed.

Is the effect constant?methodological question

Tension
One average effect can hide an effect that fades or depends on the season.
Choice
Interactions between the programme and time, and between the programme and the season.
05Work
  1. Merged three health-centre datasets exported from Kobo, then cleaned them in R: corrected dates, renamed and typed variables, reported missing records.
  2. Aggregated cases by village and week and added population as an offset, so the model estimates incidence and not raw counts.
  3. Added NASA POWER weather data and reduced it with a PCA.
  4. Fitted and compared Poisson and negative-binomial mixed models (glmmTMB), with a random effect per village.
  5. Described the raw data with weekly curves, phase comparisons (Welch, Mann-Kendall) and incidence maps by phase.
06Checks
  • Zeros1,181 weeks with zero cases observed, 1,183 predicted: no zero-inflation problem.
  • FitPosterior predictive check: simulated counts match the observed distribution.
  • VillagesVillage random effects are close to normal, as the model assumes.
  • CollinearityHigh variance inflation on the time terms (time and its interaction with the programme): their individual coefficients should be read with care.
07Outcome

≈ 42%lower malaria incidence seen in health centres once a village was covered, estimated by the model with time, season, weather and village held constant.

08Limits

What the study does not say

  • It measures cases reaching health centres, not transmission or mortality.
  • Bed-net and chemoprevention coverage were not available as covariates.
  • Satellite weather data are imprecise at village level; the season interaction has a wide interval.

What I took from it

  • The study design does as much work as the model.
  • Report the nuances (fading effect, dry season) with the same weight as the headline.

Story 3 · Event operations

What separates accepted and refused volunteer applications?

Les Vieilles Charrues
Data Science intern
Summer 2025 · Carhaix-Plouguer
Detailed results are confidential

01Context & question

Les Vieilles Charrues, one of France's largest music festivals, relies on a large volunteer workforce and receives many applications each year. The team wanted to understand which characteristics are associated with an application being accepted or refused.

Which factors are associated with accepting a volunteer application?

02Obstacle & choices

How to read profiles described by many categorical answers?methodological question

Choice
Multiple Correspondence Analysis (MCA), which maps associations between categories in a few dimensions.

Is the logistic model trustworthy?methodological question

Choice
Two different checks: Hosmer-Lemeshow for calibration (are predicted probabilities right on average?) and ROC/AUC for discrimination (does the model rank accepted above refused?).
03Work
  1. Automated the preprocessing: duplicates, missing values, recoding of answers, new variables.
  2. Explored the profiles with descriptive statistics and visualisations.
  3. Mapped the profile structure with MCA.
  4. Modelled acceptance with a logistic regression, then evaluated it.
04Outcome
05Limits

What remains open

  • A moderate AUC suggests that important drivers of the decision are not in the application data.
  • Figures beyond the AUC are confidential.

What I took from it

  • Calibration and discrimination answer different questions; check both.
  • Say clearly what a model should not be used for.
